// Introduction

What This Class Does.

Rungs 2 and 3 taught agents to build AI systems they run by hand. This class puts those systems on a trigger. A new inquiry arrives, the AI drafts the reply, the CRM note, and the next task, and the agent approves from a phone. Attendees learn the three automations worth building first, the disclosure and consent rules that must be written before any bot talks to a consumer, and the 10-question red-team script that finds a bad answer before a client does. Money, contracts, and legal questions stay human. Everything else gets faster.

// Live Build

Built On Screen, Not Described.

Live demo: speed-to-lead with a human gate

Inputs
  • The five onboarding files from rung 1 loaded into the CRM's AI layer or a connected workspace
  • A test inquiry sent live from a phone to a demo number
  • The Speed-to-Lead Job Card (below), which the automation runs on every new inquiry
  • A recorded demo call from an AI voice assistant, with its transcript
The card the room sees
AUTOMATION: Speed-to-Lead Reply TRIGGER: A new inquiry arrives by text, web form, or portal email. DRAFT: Using my five files, write a reply under 80 words in my voice. Acknowledge the specific property or question. Ask one qualifying question. Offer two time windows to talk. Disclose that an assistant drafted it if my brokerage policy requires it. ALSO DRAFT: A CRM note (source, property, stated timeline, stated area, financing status if stated). A next task with a date. QUEUE: Put the draft, note, and task in my approval queue. Notify my phone. APPROVE: I read it. I edit or approve. Nothing sends until I do. SEND: On approval, send the reply and file the note and task. LOG: Record the trigger time, draft time, approval time, and send time. Flag any draft I edited so I can fix the rule. NEVER: Quote price flexibility, seller motivation, commission terms, legal or tax opinions, or anything about who lives in the neighborhood. Never promise a showing time. Never text a number that has opted out.
What comes out
  • A drafted reply, CRM note, and task sitting in an approval queue within 60 seconds of the test inquiry
  • One-tap approval from a phone, then the send and the log entry
  • A voice call transcript handed into the CRM with the four qualifying answers captured
  • The red-team scorecard: ten questions, ten passing answers, and one deliberately failing answer so the room sees the difference

// Safeguards

The Rules That Do Not Move.

  • Bot disclosure: California requires a clear, conspicuous disclosure when a bot communicates with a person online for commercial purposes. The templates say it plainly and early.
  • Texting consent: marketing texts need prior express written consent. Keep the opt-in record. Honor STOP immediately. Your CRM should enforce both.
  • Broker supervision: DRE guidance is clear that the broker supervises licensed activity. An automated message is still your message and your broker's responsibility.
  • Fair housing: the bot answers neighborhood, school, and demographic questions with sourced facts or a hand-off, never an opinion. The red-team script tests this directly.
  • Money, contracts, legal: the bot never discusses price flexibility, commission, contract terms, or legal questions. It hands off. That rule is written before go-live, not after.
  • Privacy: consumer data captured by the bot lives in the CRM under your brokerage's policy, not in a public consumer AI.

Every rung of the series runs the S.A.F.E. gate before anything leaves the office. Sensitive information removed. Accuracy confirmed through reliable sources. Fair housing and fiduciary responsibilities protected. Everything properly disclosed and approved.

// FAQ

Questions Agents Ask.

Do I need a specific CRM?

No. Most CRM platforms sold to agents now ship an automation layer, and the loop is the same on all of them. The class shows one and names the equivalents. Bring whichever your brokerage supports.

Is an AI answering my phone legal in California?

Yes, with disclosure and consent handled correctly. The class covers the bot disclosure requirement, texting consent, and DRE supervision. The templates in the kit are written to meet them. Confirm with your broker before go-live.

Will clients hate talking to a bot?

Clients hate voicemail more. A disclosed assistant that answers on the first ring, asks four good questions, and books a call with you beats a callback at 9 AM. The red-team script keeps it from saying anything that would embarrass you.

Can I skip the approval gate once it works?

Not for anything client-facing that touches money, contracts, or legal questions. For low-risk sends, rung 5 covers act-within-limits automation and how to earn your way there.
