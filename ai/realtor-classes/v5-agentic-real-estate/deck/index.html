Chatbot versus agent

The moment AI stops writing and starts doing.

A chatbot

Returns text. You copy it somewhere. Nothing happens until you act.

An agent

Uses tools. Reads a folder. Browses for a source. Drafts across email and calendar. Runs a multi-step task. Can send, book, and move files.

Where it exists today

Every major AI platform ships an agent mode: file reading, browsing, connectors to mail, calendar, and drive, and computer-use modes.

Why the risk changed

A wrong paragraph is a bad draft. A wrong action is a sent email, a moved file, a booked appointment. So we ladder the permissions.

The Permission Ladder

Four rungs. Nothing client-facing runs unsupervised.

  1. Read-only. Summarize the escrow folder. Find the deadline. Compare two documents.
  2. Draft-only. Write the seller update. Write the repair response for my review.
  3. Act-with-approval. Put the contingency deadline on the calendar. I click yes.
  4. Act-within-limits. Send the Monday showing report to the seller if nothing changed and the checklist passed. Earned after a clean month.
Never

No rung reaches these.

  • Sign anything
  • Send an offer, a counter, or a contract document
  • Give legal or tax advice, or interpret contract language beyond quoting it
  • Contact any party about money
  • Move, delete, or share a client file outside the approved workspace
The brokerage intelligence layer

A private place the agent reads from.

What goes in

Office policies. Forms guidance: how your brokerage fills what. Local knowledge with sources: HOAs, assessment districts, school boundaries. The approved sources list. The prohibited list.

Where it lives

A private workspace with model training on your data switched off and retention set per brokerage policy. Not a public consumer chat.

Six questions for any vendor

Encryption. Retention. Training on our data. Admin controls. Audit logs. Data location.

Who maintains it

One owner. A monthly review. New agents onboard to the same layer in an afternoon.

The card the room sees

Transaction Desk, with the rung on every step.

AGENT JOB: Transaction Desk PERMISSIONS: Read-only on the escrow folder. Draft-only for any message. Act-with-approval for calendar entries and reminders. No sending. No signing. No contact with any party. STEP 1 (read-only): Read the folder. Summarize the transaction in ten lines: parties by role only, acceptance date, price as stated, contingencies and their day counts, open items. STEP 2 (read-only): Build the task list keyed to the acceptance date. One row per deadline: task, due date, who owns it, source document. STEP 3 (draft-only): Draft the seller update in my voice using only the folder. Label anything not in the folder MISSING. STEP 4 (act-with-approval): Propose calendar entries for each contingency deadline with a reminder two business days prior. Ask me to approve each one. Add only what I approve. STEP 5 (log): List every file you read, every draft you produced, every action you proposed, and every approval you received. NEVER: Give legal or tax advice. Interpret contract language beyond quoting it. Contact any party. Move, delete, or share any file. Act on anything outside this folder.
Live build

Back to the folder. Watch for four things.

  • Read-only: the ten-line summary and the task list keyed to Day 0.
  • Draft-only: the seller update, with MISSING where the folder is silent.
  • Act-with-approval: it proposes the inspection contingency on the calendar and waits. I approve one. I reject one.
  • The log: every file read, every draft, every proposal, every approval.
Team and brokerage deployment

Roles, retention, supervision.

Roles

Who may run which rung. Assistants and coordinators on read-only and draft-only. Agents on act-with-approval. Act-within-limits by written exception.

Settings

Training off. Retention per policy. Audit logs on. A dedicated account for the agent where possible.

Broker supervision

An agent running under a licensee's login is that licensee's activity. The broker reviews the log the way they review a file.

Onboarding

A new agent gets the five files template, the brokerage layer, and the permission card. One afternoon.

The monthly audit

Fifteen minutes. Three numbers and a review.

Hours recovered

Estimate per task, times runs. Times your hourly value. That is the number to watch.

Errors caught

Drafts rejected, proposals rejected, and why. Each one becomes a rule.

Permissions reviewed

Did any task earn a rung? Did any lose one?

Access hygiene

What is connected. What was switched off. Who has the login.

The 12-month plan

One year. Rung by rung.

  1. Q1. Rungs 1 and 2 for every agent. Five files and one Job Card each.
  2. Q2. The listing system. One fact sheet per listing, the chain, the critique.
  3. Q3. One automation live with the gate on. Red-teamed. Measured.
  4. Q4. An agent on read-only and draft-only. One act-with-approval task earned.
The seven-day challenge

One week. One thing a day.

  1. Place five tasks you do every week on the Permission Ladder.
  2. Ask your platform vendor the six questions. In writing.
  3. Build the brokerage layer starter: policies, one forms guide, one local knowledge page with sources.
  4. Run the Transaction Desk on the fictional escrow folder, read-only.
  5. Run the draft-only step. Read every MISSING.
  6. Run act-with-approval on a test calendar. Approve one. Reject one. Read the log.
  7. Write your 12-month plan. Put the monthly audit on the calendar.
