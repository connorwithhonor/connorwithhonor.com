// Introduction

What This Class Does.

The first four rungs taught agents to onboard an AI, give it a job, connect the jobs, and put them on a trigger. This class covers the layer that arrived in force in 2025 and 2026: AI agents that do not just write, they act. They read a folder, browse for a source, draft across email and calendar, and run a multi-step task. Attendees learn the four-rung Permission Ladder that decides what an agent may do on its own, build a private brokerage intelligence layer, and watch a fictional escrow file become a task list, a seller update, a deadline flag, and calendar reminders, with approval at every act. Then they leave with a monthly audit and a 12-month plan for a solo agent, a team, and a brokerage.

// Live Build

Built On Screen, Not Described.

Live build: the Transaction Desk

Inputs
  • The five onboarding files from rung 1 and the brokerage intelligence layer built in this class
  • A fictional escrow folder: purchase agreement summary, six invented emails, an inspection report cover page, and the acceptance date, all labeled fictional
  • The Transaction Desk Job Card (below), with the permission rung stated for every step
  • A connected calendar in a test account
The card the room sees
AGENT JOB: Transaction Desk PERMISSIONS: Read-only on the escrow folder. Draft-only for any message. Act-with-approval for calendar entries and reminders. No sending. No signing. No contact with any party. STEP 1 (read-only): Read the folder. Summarize the transaction in ten lines: parties by role only, acceptance date, price as stated, contingencies and their day counts, open items. STEP 2 (read-only): Build the task list keyed to the acceptance date. One row per deadline: task, due date, who owns it, source document. STEP 3 (draft-only): Draft the seller update in my voice using only the folder. Label anything not in the folder MISSING. STEP 4 (act-with-approval): Propose calendar entries for each contingency deadline with a reminder two business days prior. Ask me to approve each one. Add only what I approve. STEP 5 (log): List every file you read, every draft you produced, every action you proposed, and every approval you received. NEVER: Give legal or tax advice. Interpret contract language beyond quoting it. Contact any party. Move, delete, or share any file. Act on anything outside this folder.
What comes out
  • A ten-line transaction summary and a dated task list, produced read-only
  • A seller update draft with MISSING labels where the folder was silent
  • Calendar proposals for each contingency deadline, approved one at a time on screen
  • The action log: files read, drafts produced, actions proposed, approvals received

// Safeguards

The Rules That Do Not Move.

  • Confidentiality: escrow files contain protected information. They go only into an approved private workspace with training disabled and retention set, never into a public consumer AI.
  • Permissions: nothing client-facing runs unsupervised. Act-within-limits is earned after a documented month of act-with-approval with no errors, and only for low-risk sends.
  • No unauthorized practice of law: the agent quotes contract language and dates. It does not interpret. Legal questions go to the broker or counsel.
  • Broker supervision: an agent running under a licensee's login is that licensee's activity and the broker's supervisory responsibility. Audit logs exist so supervision is possible.
  • California DRE AI Advisory: supervision, advertising, privacy, fair housing, altered images, and legal interpretation. The Permission Ladder maps to each.
  • Access hygiene: before connecting an agent to email or calendar, switch off what it does not need, use a dedicated account where possible, and review the log weekly for the first month.

Every rung of the series runs the S.A.F.E. gate before anything leaves the office. Sensitive information removed. Accuracy confirmed through reliable sources. Fair housing and fiduciary responsibilities protected. Everything properly disclosed and approved.

// FAQ

Questions Agents Ask.

Is this safe for a small brokerage?

Safer than what most small brokerages do now, which is agents pasting client data into free chat tools. The private layer, the permission ladder, and the audit log give the broker visibility they do not have today.

Do I need to be technical?

No. The class runs the build on screen with plain-language cards. If you can set up a shared drive folder, you can do this.

What does an agent cost to run?

Enterprise and team tiers on the major platforms are priced per seat per month. The class does not sell a platform. It gives you the six vendor questions and the permission ladder so the choice is yours.

How long until the agent runs on its own?

It does not. Act-within-limits is the ceiling, and it is earned one low-risk task at a time after a clean month of approvals. The licensee stays in command. That is the design, not a limitation.
