// Introduction

What This Class Does.

Rung 2 taught agents to give the AI one job. This class connects the jobs. Attendees build a Property Fact Sheet, the single verified source of truth for a listing, then chain Job Cards so that one sheet produces every output a listing launch needs. A separate critique job checks the outputs for fair housing and accuracy before anything is published. Change one fact, re-run, and every output updates. No retyping, no drift.

// Who It Is For

Come If This Is You.

  • You completed rung 2, or you have built and run at least one AI Job Card on your own.
  • You take listings, or you support an agent who does (transaction coordinators and marketing assistants welcome).
  • You have retyped the same square footage into five places and gotten it wrong once.
// Outcomes

You Leave Able To.

  1. Build a Property Fact Sheet: one verified source for a listing with facts, sources, approved photos, disclosure status, and an explicit UNKNOWN list.
  2. Chain Job Cards into a Listing Launch System that produces ten outputs from that one sheet.
  3. Run a fair housing and accuracy critique as its own job, before anything is published.
  4. Change one fact and regenerate every output in minutes, without retyping.
  5. Store the system in a workspace so it runs the same way for the next listing and the next person on your team.
// What We Cover

The Eight Points.

  • Why one prompt at a time breaks the moment a second listing comes in: drift, retyping, and the wrong fact in the wrong place
  • The Property Fact Sheet: the single verified source every output must cite, with a source next to every fact
  • Chaining Job Cards: MLS remarks, public remarks, three social captions, sphere email, just-listed text, open house plan, buyer-agent FAQ, and a neighborhood one-pager
  • Live build: fact sheet in, ten outputs out, in under 15 minutes
  • The Critique Job: the AI checks its own outputs against a fair housing and accuracy rule list
  • Version control for humans: change the fact sheet, re-run, every output updates
  • Photos, virtual staging, and altered images: what must be disclosed and how to label it
  • Storing the system so it survives to the next listing and the next agent on your team

// Live Build

Built On Screen, Not Described.

Live build: the Listing Launch System

Inputs
  • The five onboarding files from rung 1
  • A fictional Property Fact Sheet: a Valencia single-family home with invented facts (4 bed, 2.5 bath, 2,140 square feet per fictional tax record, built 1998, HOA and Mello-Roos status listed with fictional sources), a photo list with alteration status, disclosure status, and an UNKNOWN list
  • Ten Job Cards, one per output, each inheriting the stop rules from rung 2
  • The Critique Job Card with its rule list
The card the room sees
JOB: Listing Launch (chain) SOURCE OF TRUTH: The Property Fact Sheet pasted below. No output may state a fact that is not on the sheet. If a fact is missing, write MISSING. OUTPUTS, IN ORDER: 1. MLS remarks (per my MLS's remark rules, no contact info, no offers of compensation). 2. Public remarks. 3. Three social captions (short, medium, long). 4. Sphere email. 5. Just-listed text under 60 words. 6. Open house plan with a visitor question list. 7. Buyer-agent FAQ (showing instructions, offer logistics, HOA and disclosure availability). 8. Neighborhood one-pager using only sheet facts and sourced links. 9. Seller weekly update template. 10. Photo caption set with alteration labels where the sheet says altered or virtually staged. THEN RUN: Critique Job on all ten. Return a table: output number, issue, rule broken, suggested fix. STOP RULES: No superlatives without a sheet fact behind them. No school quality claims. No neighborhood demographic language. No price or motivation commentary. Brokerage name and license identification appear where my advertising rules require it. Nothing publishes without my review.
What comes out
  • Ten outputs, generated in sequence, all citing the same sheet
  • A critique table with the planted steering line caught and the fix pointed at the sheet
  • A second full run after one fact changes, showing all ten outputs updated
  • The workspace layout: files, fact sheet, cards, and a naming convention for the next listing

// Safeguards

The Rules That Do Not Move.

  • MLS remark rules: public remarks follow your MLS's rules on contact information, compensation, and prohibited content. The Job Card carries the rule and the critique checks it.
  • Fair housing advertising: no steering, no protected-class language, no school quality claims dressed up as amenities. HUD advertising guidance is the word list the Critique Job uses.
  • Altered images: virtual staging, sky replacement, and object removal are labeled on the fact sheet and disclosed in the caption set. The DRE AI Advisory covers altered images. Your MLS may have its own rule.
  • Copyright: listing photos belong to the photographer unless the license says otherwise. The sheet records the license.
  • Advertising identification: brokerage name and license identification appear where California advertising rules require them, including first point of contact materials.
  • Confidentiality: the seller's disclosures and private communications do not go into a public consumer AI. The fact sheet carries what the seller approved for marketing, nothing else.

Every rung of the series runs the S.A.F.E. gate before anything leaves the office. Sensitive information removed. Accuracy confirmed through reliable sources. Fair housing and fiduciary responsibilities protected. Everything properly disclosed and approved.

// FAQ

Questions Agents Ask.

I do not take many listings. Is this still useful?

Yes. The same pattern runs a buyer tour packet, a rental listing, or a team's weekly market update. The listing launch is the teaching example because every agent understands it.

What if my brokerage supplies the marketing?

Bring the brokerage's templates. The system feeds them. The fact sheet still saves the retyping and the critique still catches the fair housing miss before it ships.

Does this replace my marketing person?

No. It gives your marketing person a verified sheet and ten clean first drafts instead of a text message with three facts and a typo.

Which platform runs the chain best?

Any platform with a project or workspace feature runs it. The class shows one and names the equivalents on the others.
