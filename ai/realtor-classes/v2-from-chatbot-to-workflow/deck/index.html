Rung 1 in 90 seconds

Five files. One brief. One gate.

Five files

Who I am. How I sound. How I work. What I know. What I will not do.

The briefing prompt

Objective, audience, verified facts, boundaries, format, a verification list at the end.

Train through correction

Brief, Draft, Critique, Correct, Save. Name the defect, give the rule, save the rule.

S.A.F.E.

Sensitive removed. Accurate. Fair. Everything disclosed and approved.

The shift

An AI that knows you
versus an AI that does one job the same way every time.

Pick the workflow

Five signals that a task is asking to be a workflow.

  1. Repeat. What happens every week?
  2. Wait. Where does work stall?
  3. Search. What do you keep hunting for?
  4. Rewrite. What do you explain again and again?
  5. Handoff. Where does information disappear between people or systems?
The low-risk test

Five checks before anything becomes a workflow.

  1. Does the draft require a license to produce? No.
  2. Does it touch confidential data? Not with fictional practice data, and not until the brokerage approves the platform.
  3. Is the source reliable? Your own sign-in sheet and your own fact sheet.
  4. Is the result reversible? It is a draft. Nothing sends.
  5. Who approves before a client sees it? You. Every time.
The AI Job Card

Five parts. Every job, every time.

  1. Outcome. What done looks like, in one sentence, with a time.
  2. Approved inputs. What it may use. Nothing else. Missing means MISSING.
  3. Required steps. Numbered. In order. No skipping.
  4. Output format. The exact shape, so you can scan it in 20 seconds.
  5. Stop and approval rules. What it must never do, and who approves.
The card the room sees

Open House Follow-Up, filled in.

JOB: Open House Follow-Up OUTCOME: Every visitor from today's open house gets a personal follow-up draft, a CRM note, and a next step, ready for my review within 20 minutes of closing the door. APPROVED INPUTS: My five files. The sign-in sheet I paste in. The listing fact sheet I paste in. Nothing else. If a fact is not in these, label it MISSING. REQUIRED STEPS: 1. Summarize each visitor in one line. 2. Rank by buying signals stated in their notes only. 3. Draft one text (under 60 words) and one email (under 120 words) per visitor in my voice. 4. Write a CRM note per visitor: source, timeline, area, financing status if stated, next step. 5. Write call prep for the top two: three questions to ask, one thing not to assume. 6. Recommend tasks with dates. OUTPUT FORMAT: One block per visitor in this order: Summary, Rank, Text draft, Email draft, CRM note, Task. Then: Call prep (top two). Then: FACTS TO VERIFY. Then: COMPLIANCE CHECK. STOP AND APPROVAL RULES: Do not infer age, family status, religion, national origin, disability, or any protected characteristic from names or notes. Do not mention price flexibility, seller motivation, or offer strategy. Do not promise showings or availability. Do not send anything. I review every draft before it goes out.
Stop rules

Three rules that never move.

  • Never infer age, family status, religion, national origin, disability, or any protected characteristic from a name or a note.
  • Never mention price flexibility, seller motivation, or offer strategy.
  • Never send. You review every draft.
Live build

Switching to the workspace. Watch for four things.

  • The five files and the card load into one project workspace.
  • A fictional sign-in sheet with eight visitors goes in. Names invented. Numbers are 555.
  • It returns one block per visitor in the exact format, then call prep, then two lists.
  • The FACTS TO VERIFY and COMPLIANCE CHECK lists at the end. Those are the point.
What came out

Eight visitors. One shape.

  • Summary, rank, text draft, email draft, CRM note, task. Same order, every visitor.
  • Call prep for the top two: three questions, one thing not to assume.
  • FACTS TO VERIFY: everything it was not told.
  • COMPLIANCE CHECK: what it flagged in its own drafts.
Break it on purpose

Feed it a trap. Watch what it does.

Two planted notes: one visitor asked if this is a good area for kids. One asked if the seller would take less.

The right answer to both is a hand-off, not an opinion. If it guesses, we write the rule and run it again.
The habit

Brief. Draft. Critique. Correct. Save.

  1. Brief with the card.
  2. Draft on fictional data first.
  3. Critique: name the defect, not 'make it better'.
  4. Correct with a rule, one sentence.
  5. Save the rule to file five so it never happens again.
Rules that do not move

The safeguards, every run.

  • Privacy: real visitor data goes only where the brokerage approved the platform and settings.
  • Fair housing: no inference, no steering. HUD advertising guidance is the word list.
  • Verification: every property fact confirmed outside the model.
  • Licensee responsibility: the AI drafts, you approve, nothing sends itself.
  • DRE AI Advisory: supervision, advertising, privacy, fair housing, altered images, legal interpretation.

The seven-day challenge

One week. One thing a day.

  1. Pick the workflow. Run the five-signal audit and the low-risk test.
  2. Write the Job Card. All five parts.
  3. Build the workspace. Five files plus the card.
  4. Run it on fictional data.
  5. Break it on purpose. Write the correction as a rule.
  6. Save the rules to file five. Run it clean.
  7. First real run, with your review on every draft.
