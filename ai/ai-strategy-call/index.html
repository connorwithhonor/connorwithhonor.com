What a 30-minute AI strategy call with me covers

A 30-minute AI strategy call with me starts with a task your business needs to handle better. I ask how it works now, where it breaks, and who owns the result. We narrow the opportunity and identify a next step, including what needs checking before any build can begin.

Who should book this conversation?

This call is for an owner or team lead who wants to make a practical AI decision. You might have a pile of product suggestions and no clear starting point. You might have tried a tool that creates drafts but leaves staff doing the same cleanup afterward.

Bring the person who can describe the work and help decide what may change. A Santa Clarita owner between appointments should not need to prepare a technical presentation. A plain description of a recurring task is enough to begin. I use the conversation to identify a useful question, not to pretend we can solve every process at once.

What should I bring without exposing private information?

Choose a task and think through what starts it, who touches it, and where the result goes. If you have a sample, remove customer names, contact details, private notes, and anything unnecessary to the discussion. Describe sensitive steps without opening the underlying record.

Bring the names of the tools already involved and explain what staff dislike about the current routine. Do not send passwords or access codes. I do not need to enter your accounts to understand the initial problem. If later work requires access, we define that separately after deciding what the work actually includes.

How does the process audit fit into the call?

I begin by asking you to walk through the task as it happens today. I listen for missing information, repeated entry, unclear responsibility, and decisions that depend on experience. Those details help separate a process problem from a software problem.

This is an initial audit conversation, not a completed technical inspection. I cannot verify every connection or data source during the call. I can identify what needs inspection and why it matters. If the process itself is undefined, our next step may be writing it down. Choosing a tool before that would only give the confusion another place to live.

What questions will you ask about AI and automation?

I ask what outcome you want and what would count as a bad result. Who checks the work? Where does the approved information come from? What action would the system take after producing an answer? These questions reveal how much authority the proposed tool would need.

We also discuss whether a fixed rule would do the job. A predictable assignment may not require AI at all. You can read about choosing a useful AI problem before we speak. The call stays grounded in the task you bring, with product choices following the process rather than directing it.

What will you rule out during the conversation?

I will push back on a plan that makes the software responsible for decisions only you can authorize. I will not recommend an unattended system that makes commitments from incomplete information. I also will not treat a request for advice as permission to contact your customers or change your business settings.

If the proposed task involves sensitive judgment, we can discuss preparing information for a qualified person instead. If the business cannot identify an approved source, we need to address that gap. The call should expose these limits early, while changing direction is still a conversation instead of a rebuild.

What do I leave with when the call ends?

Before we finish, I recap the problem in plain language and confirm that I have understood it. We identify the proposed next action, the person who would own it, and the information needed to make that action useful. We distinguish what you told me from what still needs verification.

You leave with a bounded starting point and a decision you can explain to staff. That may be a process correction, a sample test, or a deeper review. It is not a completed system specification or a promise of a particular result. If the information is insufficient, the next action is to gather it.

How do we discuss testing before there is a build?

I ask what examples would prove the idea useful and what mistakes would make it unacceptable. We can outline a trial using redacted material and a review step. We also identify the destination that would need checking if the proposed workflow changes records or prepares messages.

A test plan needs more than a successful answer on a screen. It needs a person who can judge accuracy and a way to stop the trial. During the call, we identify those requirements. Detailed test cases come later if we agree to further work and can inspect the actual process.

What decisions remain mine after we talk?

You decide whether to pursue the recommendation, who participates, and what information can be used. The conversation does not grant ongoing access or approval to launch anything. You keep control over the business rule and the amount of operational change you are willing to consider.

I explain the distinction between advice and execution. My small business consulting page covers a closer look at priorities. My implementation service describes building and testing an agreed workflow. Either path should follow a defined need, with its own scope, instead of being assumed when you book a conversation.

How can I make the conversation useful for my staff?

Ask the person doing the task what they wish the owner understood about it. Bring that observation into the call. An employee may know that the source document is outdated, the notification goes unread, or a required detail never arrives. That information can change the recommendation.

Afterward, share the agreed problem and next action in ordinary language. Staff should know what is being investigated and what still works as before. For more context on how I approach AI, visit my AI overview. The value of this call is clarity you can use when you return to the working day.

What else should I know before we begin?

Is the call really 30 minutes?

The booking is for a 30-minute conversation. I focus it on a business task and the next useful decision. Work that needs a deeper inspection should be scoped separately rather than squeezed into an incomplete review.

Do I need a finished AI plan?

No. Bring a recurring problem and describe the current routine. I can help narrow the question. If you already have a plan, we can examine its assumptions and identify what requires evidence.

Will you log into my systems during the call?

The initial conversation does not require account access. Describe the process or use a redacted example. Any later access should have a defined purpose, an approved permission level, and a separate agreement about the work.

Can we discuss an idea that may not need AI?

Yes. I compare the idea with simpler process changes and existing features. If the task is better handled with a fixed rule or a clearer responsibility, that belongs in the recommendation.

Does booking authorize a project?

Booking authorizes the conversation. It does not approve installation, customer messages, or a release. If further work makes sense, we define the objective, boundaries, and next action before proceeding.
