How To Set Up Claude AI Safely. And The Toggle That Does Not Mean Private.

August 22, 2026 // Episode 156
TL;DR Creating a free Claude account takes about four minutes. Buried inside those four minutes are three decisions nobody slows down to explain: how to land on the real site instead of a copycat, a pricing screen that has already picked an answer for you, and a privacy switch that most people read exactly backwards. Turning off the training toggle does not mean your words stayed home. Here is the whole walkthrough, plus the four things worth typing the moment you get inside.

The Machine

Somebody asked me how to get one of these AI systems running without stepping on a landmine. Reasonable question, and the answer is shorter than people expect. The setup is four minutes. There is nothing to install and nothing you can break.

But there are three or four decisions inside those four minutes that nobody slows down for, and one of them is the most misunderstood setting in consumer AI right now. So I recorded the whole thing, screen and all, and made a new free account live so nobody has to take my word for how it looks.

I teach real estate agents. Before that I spent 20 Yrs LAPD teaching officers. Same lesson both rooms: the technology is almost never the hard part. Somebody just has to walk it through once at a normal speed, without making anybody feel slow for asking.

The Search Bar Is Where People Get Taken

The address is claude.ai. Type it in the address bar at the very top of the browser, not the search box in the middle of the page.

That distinction is the whole security lesson. When you search for a tool instead of navigating to it, you get a page that mixes the real destination with paid ads, review blogs, and products that borrowed the name because the name carries traffic. claude.ai is correct. claude.com is Anthropic as well, the company that builds Claude, so that one is legitimate too. Keep scrolling past those and it starts drifting into things that are not.

Nobody gets taken by anything sophisticated here. They get taken by a page that looks close enough and asks for a card.

The free account never asks for a credit card. That single fact is your tripwire. If a page wants payment details before it will make you a free account, close it and type the address yourself.

Then bookmark it, and you never have to find it again.

Signing Up, And The Agreement Nobody Reads

Two doors. If you already have Gmail, Continue with Google is the fast one and there is no real downside to it. If you do not, type any email address and Claude sends you a verification link. Open your email, click the link, and it finishes the job.

Then come the terms of service and the acceptable use policy.

Almost nobody reads them. Read the shape of them at least. The acceptable use policy is genuinely readable and it tells you what the system will and will not participate in. If a person sets out to use one of these tools to hurt somebody, that gets noticed and it gets handled.

And a word to my own generation, because this came up when I taught this live. We still carry an assumption that privacy is the default and that somebody is guarding it for us. That stopped matching reality a long time ago. Not at this signup screen. Somewhere back at the phone, or the smart TV, or the twenty page agreement nobody scrolled. Knowing what you agreed to is not paranoia. It is being awake.

The Pricing Screen Has Already Picked An Answer

Three tiers show up: Free, Pro, and Max.

Watch the Pro number. When that page loads, the annual plan is already selected, and the price it displays is the yearly rate divided by twelve. Pay for the year up front and it works out to roughly 17 dollars a month. Pay month to month and it is 20.

I do not think anybody built that to trick you. It is how nearly every subscription on the internet presents itself now. But there is a difference between seeing it happen and having it happen to you. Find the monthly and annual toggle, click it, and look at the real number for the plan you would actually pick. On the Max tier, worth knowing, there is no annual discount at all.

Start free. The free plan is not a demo. It is the real assistant with a daily ceiling, and for the first several weeks that ceiling is generous.

The way to think about usage: it works like quarters in an arcade game. You get a certain amount of play, and heavy work spends it faster. Deep research, long documents, detailed back and forth, those burn through it quicker than simple questions. When you start hitting the ceiling regularly, that is the tool telling you it earned the money. Not before.

Skip The Download. For Now.

Next screen offers the desktop app. One correction I want on the record, because I got this slightly wrong on camera: the desktop app is free on every plan, including the free one. Cost is not the reason to skip it.

Skip it anyway on day one. Use the browser. Fewer moving parts, nothing installed, nothing to uninstall if you decide this is not for you. Once it has earned a permanent spot in your week, then install the desktop app or put it on your phone.

When you do go to the phone: search Claude in the App Store or Play Store and check that the maker says Anthropic. Lookalike apps sit right next to the real one wearing similar names and similar icons. Read the name on the badge before you believe the uniform.

The Toggle Almost Everybody Reads Backwards

Here is the one I actually made this video for.

You will see a switch asking whether your conversations can be used to help improve the model. Turn it off. That is the right instinct and I turned mine off too.

Now understand what you did and what you did not do.

People flip that switch and conclude their information is sealed. It is not. That toggle governs one specific thing: whether your conversations get used to train future versions of the model. It does not govern where your words went.

Anything you type into any AI system that is not running on your own machine leaves your building. It travels to a server somebody else owns. It sits there. It is reachable by subpoena. That is true with the switch on and true with the switch off.

The only genuinely private setup is a model running on hardware you own, and that means real hardware. NVIDIA sells a desktop AI machine called the DGX Spark around 5,000 dollars. AMD has a comparable box nearer 5,700. A loaded Mac Studio lands in the same neighborhood. Those run a model locally, where nothing leaves the room.

For almost everybody watching, that is not the setup, and that is fine. You are using a system that belongs to somebody else. Just operate like you know it.

So the working rule is simple. Everyday questions, drafting, research, learning, planning, all of that, go ahead. Social Security numbers, bank and account numbers, passwords, executed contracts you are not free to share, anything under medical or credit or financial privacy law, none of it, ever. Not because this company is out to get you. Because that is street sense on any website.

I went deeper on the data side of this in You Are Training AI For Free. The tool is not the advantage. What you own and what you feed it is the advantage.

Introduce Yourself, Then Overrule Yourself

Claude asks your name and what you do. Writer, educator, engineer, health care, finance, legal, student, founder, consultant, scientist, and a long list past those, including Other.

Pick something. It is not a commitment. That choice shapes how it talks to you and what it offers. Pick educator and it leans toward explaining concepts. Pick operations and it starts offering to draft procedures.

Here is the part worth knowing. You can overrule it at any time by simply saying so. On camera I told it I had made a mistake, I was not an educator, I was a rocket scientist, and it turned on a dime. No settings menu, no hunting. You talk, it adjusts.

That is the thing to absorb about this whole category. It is not software with buttons you have to locate. It is closer to working with a very sharp assistant who listens.

The First Four Things To Type

You are in. There is a box at the bottom. Start here, in this order.

One, ask it how to work with it. Type: Hello, can you tell me the best way to use you? It will tell you. Be specific about context, iterate instead of starting over, bring it a whole workflow instead of one isolated question. Straight from the tool.

Two, teach it who you are. Type: Can you help me teach you about how I like to do things? This one matters more than it looks if you are going to produce anything with your name on it. The gap between generic AI slop and something that sounds like a person is almost entirely this step, and almost nobody does it.

Three, ask what the buttons mean. Projects, Artifacts, Code, Customize. Do not go hunting for a manual. Ask it. It will explain its own interface.

Four, make it come down to your level. This is the most useful sentence I have ever handed anybody: Explain that to me like I am in the 7th or 8th grade.

There is no insult in it. Strip out the jargon and the light comes on dramatically faster. I ask for that on subjects I already know well, because the plain version is usually the version that sticks. Ask as many times as you want. It never sighs, never rushes you, never makes anybody feel slow.

That patience is not a side effect. It is the whole reason this thing works for people who spent thirty years being told they were bad with computers.

What To Do Tonight

Go to claude.ai. Make the free account. Skip the download. Turn the training toggle off. Pick any role. Ask it one real question about something actually on your plate this week. When the answer comes back too technical, tell it to explain like you are in the 8th grade.

Common Questions

What is the real Claude website?

claude.ai is the real one, and claude.com is also Anthropic, the company that builds Claude, so both are legitimate. The safest habit is typing claude.ai directly into the address bar at the top of your browser rather than searching for it, because a search results page mixes the real site with paid ads, review blogs, and copycat tools that borrowed the name. If any page demands a credit card just to create a free account, you are in the wrong place. Back out and type the address yourself.

Does turning off the training toggle make my chats private?

No, and this is the most misread setting in consumer AI. Turning it off tells the company not to use your conversations to improve their models. It does not mean your words never left your building. They still travel to a server somebody else owns, they are still stored, and they can still be reached by subpoena. Turn it off anyway, it is the right instinct. Just do not mistake it for privacy. The only truly private setup is a model running on hardware you own.

Is the free plan good enough to start with?

Yes. The free plan is the real assistant with a daily usage limit, not a stripped down demo. Usage works like quarters in an arcade game: heavy research and long documents spend it faster than simple questions. When you start bumping that ceiling regularly, that is the tool telling you it earned an upgrade. Nobody needs to decide that on day one.

Why does the pricing screen show the yearly price first?

Because the annual plan is preselected and it displays the annual rate divided by twelve, which reads as a lower monthly number. Paying month to month costs more than that figure suggests. It is standard across subscriptions online rather than unique to this company, but click the monthly and annual toggle so you are comparing the real number for the plan you would actually choose. The Max tier has no annual discount at all.

Do I need to install the desktop app?

No. The desktop app is free on every plan including the free one, so cost is not the reason to skip it. Skip it anyway on day one and use the browser version. Fewer moving parts and nothing to uninstall if you decide it is not for you. Once the tool has earned a permanent place in your week, install the desktop app or the phone app then.

I am over 50 and not technical. Is this really for me?

Yes, and the setup is genuinely about four minutes. Nothing to memorize, nothing you can break by typing the wrong thing. The one habit that has to change is how you write to it. Search engines trained everybody to type three word fragments. This works the opposite way. Give it context in full sentences, the way you would brief a sharp new assistant on their first morning, and the quality of what comes back changes immediately.
