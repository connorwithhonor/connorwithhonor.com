No Human Left Behind: The Moonshot the AI Panel Missed

November 5, 2026 // AI With Honor // Connor MacIvor
TL;DR
  • The bet. Big Tech's AI pledges now top $3 trillion, by a Bloomberg tally of Alphabet, Meta, Microsoft, Amazon and Oracle.
  • The panel's word was "tough." On Moonshots episode #308, the panel said the next few years of transition are going to be tough. Tough means very different things on each side of the cushion.
  • The cushion gap. Bankrate's 2026 survey: just 30% of Americans say they would pay a $1,000 emergency from savings.
  • The moonshot. No Human Left Behind: measure progress by how many families move from $1,000 from the edge to three months of breathing room.

Underneath a two-hour AI podcast, a 41-year-old house painter left a comment. No college degree. Three kids, 17, 13 and 11. Three words: help them win.

Nobody answered him. This article is my answer, and a challenge to the people who could answer him better than I can.

I build AI systems for local businesses here in the Santa Clarita Valley. I use this stuff every day and I'm excited about it. I also spent 20 years with the LAPD, and I've been licensed since 1998, so I've watched what happens to regular families when the ground moves under them. I'm not here to sell you anything, and I'm not here to tell you AI is going to kill us all.

Two countries in one video

The show was Moonshots, with Peter Diamandis and his panel. Smart men. Builders. Investors. They've built real things, and I'm not calling anybody a villain. I listened to the whole episode, then I read the comments, and there were two different countries in that one video. The one at the table, and the one underneath it.

They opened with the money. More than $3 trillion pledged by five companies for buildings, power, chips and leases, some running for decades. The panel compared it to 10 Apollo programs. Then one of them said the payoff is complete and total world business domination, forever.

Both halves of that are worth sitting with. The money is real. And the reward goes to whoever owns the machines.

The panel also talked about investors hedging against an AI drop. Think about who gets to buy insurance on a revolution. And who doesn't.

The worker is the cost being cut

One panelist said the best AI now costs about a dollar a day for what a human worker gets done in a day, and that the price keeps falling fast. Those are his numbers, said on the show. But you don't need his exact numbers to see the direction.

If the machine does a day of work for a dollar, the worker isn't the customer anymore. The worker is the cost being cut. Nobody at that table said it that plainly.

It's already happening inside the companies building this. Anthropic, the company behind Claude, filed to go public. In that filing it says Claude wrote more than 90% of its code contributions in September. It says the value of its own internal Claude use, priced at what customers pay, passed its monthly payroll, not counting equity pay, back in April. And it says the average engineer ships more than 45 times as much code per day as in 2024.

Those engineers are doing fine. Now think about the bookkeeper. The claims processor. The person answering the phone at the escrow office. Their bosses read that filing too. Every boss has a board, every board has a spreadsheet, and the spreadsheet doesn't have a line for your kid's braces.

Tough for who?

On the show, Peter said we're about to go through a transition over the next few years, and it's going to be tough.

Tough for who?

Bankrate asks Americans every year how they'd handle a surprise bill. In January 2026, just 30% said they would pay a $1,000 emergency out of savings. A tire. A tooth. A short paycheck. A transmission.

When somebody with a few hundred million in the bank says tough, he means his stock has a bad quarter. When a family on Soledad Canyon hears tough, it means the rent check bounces, the car goes back, and somebody ends up on a cousin's couch. That's the cushion gap. The panel lives on the cushion side. Most of this country lives on the other side, and the gap is about $1,000 wide.

In 20 years of police work, I almost never saw a family fall apart because of one big thing. It was one small thing that hit when there was no cushion. A layoff in the same month the rent went up. Not because they're bad people. Because they were $1,000 short at the wrong time.

I wrote about this same panel and the uncomfortable middle before, in who pays for the uncomfortable part and the AI utopia has a rough middle. The middle is where real people live.

What the comments said

One viewer wrote that he uses AI all day, every day, and instead of getting his time back, there's just more to do. That's the oldest story in work. Email was supposed to save us time. The smartphone was supposed to save us time. Now the boss can reach you at the soccer game. The gain goes to whoever owns the clock.

Another viewer wrote that the show goes over his head about half the time. Just a common man, asking for help. Most people aren't behind because they're not smart. They're behind because nobody's talking to them at the kitchen table.

Then the panel got to your phone. Sam Altman has said PC memory is better used in data centers. The comments lit up: we want our own AI on our own computers, the same way we want our own houses. When you own, you build something. A phone that's nothing but a window into a data center is a rental.

Should you buy a house next to a data center?

A viewer asked that. Don't buy a home on a promise from a stage. Go stand on that street at night. Ask about the power lines, the water, the trucks. Look at what homes actually sold for. A factory hires the town. A data center buys power and water, hires a handful of people, and the money leaves fast.

Empathy is not a contamination

A viewer asked whether Western civilization is contaminated by empathy, and a panelist answered that we make bad decisions when one sad story moves us more than the numbers.

I wore a badge for 20 years. Statistics don't knock on doors. People do. The person in front of you in a living room at 2 in the morning is never an outlier. They're the whole reason the job exists. You can run a spreadsheet without empathy. You can't run a community without it.

One panelist said by 2028 most voters will ask their AI how to vote. Use AI to explain a ballot measure in plain English. Never let it decide your vote.

Scared people freeze

The panel laughed about a hack. In what reporters called an apparent cyberattack, supermarket freezers in several European countries were heated to about 50°C, around 122°F. One Belgian store said it lost about €100,000 of food, and weeks earlier up to 700 French stores were hit.

They laughed about melted ice cream. I didn't. If a stranger can reach into a grocery store freezer from a laptop, he can reach into your mother's phone with your voice. Voice cloning scams go after people who love somebody.

I don't want anybody walking away from this scared, because scared people freeze, and frozen people get hurt. I want us prepared.

Six moves for the rest of us

  1. Build a cushion. Even $20 a week. Sell the stuff in the garage.
  2. Protect your parents. Make a family code word this week. Anyone calls in a panic asking for money, even in your voice, they hang up and call back on the number they already know. I laid it out in the family passphrase.
  3. Get your hands on the tools. OpenAI's own LifeVal test of everyday tasks, like childcare, budgets and government paperwork, scored its December 2025 model at 49% and its September 2026 model at 81%. Use it to read the fine print on the lease and write the letter to the insurance company.
  4. If you run a small business, look at the phone. Every missed call is a missed job. The same tools cutting corporate payrolls can answer a small business phone at 7 at night while the owner's on a ladder.
  5. Look at the trades. On SemiAnalysis's new GDPx test of real expert work, the best AI scored 12%, and on finance and legal tasks nearly every model scored zero. That's a window. A data center a thousand miles away can't climb a ladder in Valencia.
  6. Talk to your kids. Teach them to use AI as a tutor, not a cheat. Have them explain it back at dinner. Teach them to check its work. Give them one skill with their hands and one with people.

The moonshot: No Human Left Behind

They do have the power. So here's a moonshot worth putting on that table. Land every family on the cushion. Call it No Human Left Behind.

Measure it the way they measure everything, with a benchmark. Not how smart the machine got this year. How many families moved from $1,000 from the edge to three months of breathing room. How many 41-year-old painters got a business that answers its phone while they work. How many 80-year-olds learned the hang-up rule before the scam call came. How many 17-year-olds walked into a trade with a tool in their hand instead of fear in their gut.

We don't have to wait for them. Check on the neighbor who's barely hanging on. Sit with your parents. Show the guy at work how to use the thing he's afraid of. That's how it's always worked in this valley. The fire comes over the hill, and neighbors show up.

The question was never whether AI is coming. It's whether it lands on a cushion or on a family.
