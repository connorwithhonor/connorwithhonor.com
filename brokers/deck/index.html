1998 versus today

1998

A pager. A fax machine. The MLS book. Index cards. The agent who won called the most people and remembered every one of them.

Today

A phone that writes, reads a 40-page disclosure packet, and remembers every client conversation. Same winning move. The cost of remembering just dropped close to zero.

Two kinds of broker

The selling broker

Still carries listings. AI buys back hours: follow-up, listing prep, market updates, deadline tracking. The win is more appointments per week without more staff.

The non-producing owner or manager

Runs the people. AI is a supervision and recruiting tool: consistent training, a written policy, cleaner files, and a recruiting pitch that says this office knows how to use it safely.

What to automate · The task-list method

Before anyone buys a tool, every agent writes down what they actually do all week. With hours.

The list is the AI plan. Nothing gets automated that is not on it.

Three buckets

Automate

AI does it, a human checks it. Follow-up drafts, market summaries, first-draft listing copy, meeting notes, checklists.

Assist

AI helps, a human drives. Pricing prep, objection practice, CMA narrative, social calendars, appointment research.

Keep human

No machine, ever. Negotiation. Fiduciary advice. Anything signed. Money talk with a client. Fair housing judgment calls.

The manager's move

Collect the lists. Find the five tasks on almost every one.

  • Build an office template for each of the five.
  • Train one per meeting.
  • That is your next quarter's training calendar, already written.
Picking tools

The big tools are converging. Pick by the task, not the logo.

  • Claude, ChatGPT and Gemini all write, read documents, search, remember instructions and work in projects.
  • If the office lives in Google Workspace or Microsoft 365, the built-in assistant may be the cheapest win.
  • Client data only on a business or team plan with training off and an admin in control.
What to protect · Six vendor questions
  1. Encryption in transit and at rest?
  2. Retention we control, delete on demand?
  3. Training on our data: off, in writing?
  4. Admin can add and remove users in one step?
  5. Audit logs the broker can read?
  6. Location of the data, and which jurisdiction?
The Platinum Card rule

You would not hand a new hire your Platinum card on day one. Do not hand it to an AI either.

Access in steps
  • 1Read onlyReads and summarizes. Changes nothing.
  • 2Draft onlyWrites. A human sends. Every time.
  • 3Act with approvalProposes a calendar entry or CRM task, waits for a click.
  • 4Act within limitsLow-risk sends only, by written exception from the broker.

Never, at any level: signing, sending offers or contracts, legal or tax advice, money talk with a client, moving client files outside approved systems.

How to find tomorrow's sellers
2,000names in a busy agent's database

Tomorrow's sellers are already in your database. Most of them have not heard from you in a year.

Who to call next · five steps
  1. Clean the list. Duplicates, missing fields, no contact in 12 months.
  2. Add public records. Years owned, owner-occupied or not, property type.
  3. Add market context. What their neighborhood did this year.
  4. Score by property, never by person.
  5. Resurrect it. A real note. AI drafts, the agent edits and sends.
MLS data · inside the rules

Safe start

Aggregate market statistics, your own listings and sales, public county records.

Off limits without asking

Solicitation lists built from MLS data, including expired and canceled listings. MLS data pasted into public AI tools. Ask CRMLS first.

What to protect · AI dialing
$1,500per call, when the TCPA is broken on purpose

An AI voice is an artificial voice under the TCPA.

  • FCC ruling, February 2024.
  • Marketing calls with an artificial voice need prior express written consent.
  • $500 to $1,500 per call. Class actions multiply it.
  • Humans dial. Scrub the Do Not Call list.
Fair housing

Target the property. Never the people.

Yes

Years owned. Estimated equity. Property type. Past relationship. Your own sold history.

No

Age, family status, race, religion, national origin, disability, or any guess about them. Not in a list, not in an ad audience, not in a neighborhood description.

Local marketing intel
  1. Timing by area. Your own insights show when each town is online. AI turns the export into a posting calendar.
  2. Where people talk. Local groups, parent groups, HOA newsletters. Follow their rules. Be useful first.
  3. Local vendors and events. A monthly list of markets, fundraisers and openings. Show up. Feature local businesses.
Reviews · the safe way to show your best

Do

Show a labeled "Featured reviews" selection with a link to your complete Google or Zillow profile right beside it.

Don't

Hide negative reviews and imply the page shows them all. Survey first and send only happy clients to Google. Google bans that outright.

Title and escrow partners

Free help is not always free. RESPA Section 8.

Generally fine

Educational classes. Event sponsorship. Standard property profiles.

Ask counsel first

A title company building your database, running your marketing, or setting up your AI, in exchange for an expectation of business.

Two California rules

Altered listing photos

Since January 1 (AB 723): disclose on or next to the image, and link to the original. Virtual staging included.

Bots say they are bots

A bot used online to influence a sale has to disclose that it is a bot.

Private and local models

Some work should never leave your building.

  • Free open models run on a good desktop. Ollama and LM Studio install them in minutes.
  • Nothing typed leaves the machine. No monthly bill.
  • The office pattern: public information anywhere. Client information on the business plan with training off, or on the local machine.
